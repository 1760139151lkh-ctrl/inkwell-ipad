PRD §6.12 · our design

The recording view

A Recordings panel on the right, opened from the waveform button in the top-right. The page stays visible and keeps replaying beside it. It replaces both Notability's playback ⋯ popover and its Transcripts tab.

Top: recordings

Name · duration · date and time. Tap a row to see its transcript; ⋯ gives Rename, Transcribe, Delete. While recording, this becomes a live card with a timer, level meter and Stop.

Middle: transcript

Timestamped lines. During playback, the current line is highlighted and the list follows the playhead. Tap any line to seek, the same seek as tapping ink.

Bottom: transport, pinned

A scrubber across the whole note, with a tick at each recording boundary. −10 / +10, play/pause, and speed from 0.5× to 2×.

PRD §6.2 – 6.3

Library

Sidebar with Settings, Search, Notes and Subjects. The note list shows the subject's serif title and rows with a thumbnail, title, date and mic glyph. Search also matches transcript text and shows the matching line.

PRD §6.4 – 6.9

Editor & tools

The toolbar is Lasso · Pen · Pencil · Highlighter · Eraser | Record · Play | Navigate. Each ink tool shows its current color as a small bar under the icon. Pages are US Letter with the title above page 1, and the page navigator sits bottom-right.

Not pictured: the note ⋯ menu (Share PDF · Paper… · Rename · Move to… · Delete Note) and the long-press menus. They're standard iPadOS menus, and the simulator can't open them without taking over your screen.

PRD §6.10 – 6.11

Paper & Settings

Settings keeps only what the PRD allows: Document, Pencil, Audio, Backup, Recently Deleted and About.

Judgment calls

Decisions I made (easy to change)

  1. Portrait library slides over the note. At 564 pt the editor column can't fit the toolbar. Landscape keeps Notability's three columns.
  2. Recordings button (waveform) in the top-right, next to ⋯ and Pages. Record and Play sit side by side in the toolbar, as the PRD specifies.
  3. × on the playback bar leaves replay mode, so ink returns to full opacity.
  4. Audio is captured crash-safe, then compressed. It records to PCM .caf and converts to AAC .m4a on stop. An .m4a file isn't readable until it's closed, so a crash would lose the recording. This is why crash recovery works.
  5. Native mic sample rate (48 kHz) instead of 44.1 kHz, so nothing is resampled on the audio thread.

Needs your real iPad and Pencil: pressure and double-tap, palm rejection, a real call played through the Mac speakers, and a partial-erase check on timed ink (M3-5). The full list is in review/README.md.
